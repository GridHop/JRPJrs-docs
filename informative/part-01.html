Cosmic-Time Generative Presentism (CTGP) proposes that only the currently generated state of the universe exists. Earlier states genuinely existed when they were present but no longer exist. Generation is gapless: reality never lapses, and each present state arises from immediately preceding states through lawful temporal and generative progression. What ceases to exist is each earlier state itself, not necessarily the physical systems that were in that state; whether those systems persist depends on their own conditions of persistence. Gapless generation does not require every physical quantity to change smoothly; abrupt changes, including quantum jumps in models that contain them, are compatible with it. The physical consequences of what previously occurred are instantiated in presently existing configurations of matter, fields, and radiation, some of which constitute present records of past events. These consequences and records are not the past itself; they are parts of the currently existing physical reality. The past consists of what actually happened during formerly present stages, while the future has not yet been generated. The paper develops this as a presentist interpretation of relativistic spacetime that leaves Einstein’s equations unchanged.
 
In the emergent geometric regime, the present is identified with the level sets of the cosmological time function: the supremum of proper time along past-directed causal curves, which in a big-bang universe is the maximal proper time elapsed since the initial singularity. By a theorem of Andersson, Galloway, and Howard, these level sets are future Cauchy surfaces whenever cosmological time is regular: every worldline that continues past a level set crosses it exactly once, so the present remains globally well defined even inside collapsed structure, where cosmological time can lose differentiability and its level sets can acquire corners. The view therefore applies insofar as the actual universe is globally hyperbolic with regular cosmological time. The present is a whole spatial slice of the universe, not a point, and what exists is the physical configuration of fields and matter on that slice. Special relativity alone supplies no such slice. It comes from the actual matter-filled cosmological solution of general relativity, fixed by its geometry rather than by any idealized observers. Nothing in cosmological time marks one slice as “now”: the geometry fixes which events are co-present, and the ontology supplies presentness. Identifying these slices with the present is the paper’s interpretive posit, not a theorem, and the present can be located from its past alone, without reference to the future. The generation parameter is a monotone relabeling of this function. It introduces no new field, no new degree of freedom, and no local preferred-frame effect, and it can optionally be expressed through a constrained action that leaves the Einstein equations unchanged.
 
CTGP is dynamic in one precise sense: the present is continuously generated rather than being one slice of a completed block. It is not dynamic in the sense of an observer-dependent or matter-dependent “now.” The paper tests whether generation can be tied to local matter density and argues that, in the classes of laws analyzed, it cannot. An exact density-weighted law drives the generation flow out of alignment with matter within a light-crossing time of any structure, and stable soft completions force a uniform rate inside bound structures, suppressing any density-weighted cosmological effect below one part in 10⁴⁰. Within these classes, the generation rate may vary with cosmic epoch but not across space, and no intrinsic cosmological axis follows.
 
On empirical standing, presentist and eternalist readings of identical physics are shown to be observationally equivalent, since every observation is an observation of present records, so the ontology cannot be verified by measurement at fixed physics. It remains exposed through a structural commitment: the present state and the fundamental laws must suffice to generate its successor, deterministically or stochastically, without indispensable future boundary data or global consistency requirements that preclude an adequate forward-generative account. Quantum randomness does not threaten this, since it bears on which successor occurs, not on whether the present suffices to produce one. Neither does history-dependent behavior such as magnetic hysteresis, whose history is carried by present physical variables. A confirmed chronology violation, interventionist retrocausal dependence, or an indispensable future boundary condition or global consistency requirement that precludes an adequate forward-generative account would refute the view. A unique final state that simply results from deterministic dynamics does not count; only a final condition that must be imposed as an independent input to fix earlier states would. Current physics is consistent with the commitment but does not establish it.
 
Also treated: the truthmaking of past-tense statements, constraints on quantum interpretation (CTGP requires a single realized outcome, which excludes standard Everettian ontology), cosmological observables as causal records, objections from point presentism and special relativity, the relationship to causal set theory and growing-block models, formal results, and an optional extension to conscious experience that the core framework does not presuppose.
 
Identifies the present with global cosmic-time slices; treats observations as present physical records; acknowledges observational equivalence with eternalism at fixed physics; and remains vulnerable to confirmed chronology violations, interventionist retrocausality, or indispensable future boundary conditions or global consistency requirements that preclude an adequate forward-generative account.
 
Our memories and the physical things we access to remember anything are always presently existing things, such as brain states and records resulting from the causal consequences of earlier events. With every moment that passes, more events become past events. The past does not exist; what is now past existed when it was present. It is not as though the past never was. It genuinely happened, and it remains objectively true that it happened, whether or not evidence of it is still available. The past is what was formerly present, while the future is what has not yet been generated.
 
What can be used to infer or gain knowledge about the past is not the past itself. Knowledge and information about the past, including memories and records, are presently existing physical or informational states, not earlier states that continue to exist. The past itself does not exist as any present physical entity, not even as data traces or memories. However, present physical consequences of earlier events, including records and memories, can be used to infer what happened.
 
Temporal and generative progression is gapless: physical reality never lapses. Each physical component or system persists through changes for as long as its own conditions of persistence are satisfied, that is, until it undergoes its end, if it is the kind of thing that can end. The cessation of an earlier state does not, by itself, entail the cessation of the component or system whose state it was. Individual components and systems may come into existence or cease to exist, while physical reality’s lawful temporal and generative progression continues without interruption. Gaplessness does not require every physical quantity to change smoothly.
 
Quantum reference frames (QRFs) permit different quantum systems to serve as reference frames, raising a foundational question for single-outcome theories: how can frame-dependent descriptions represent one objectively realized history? Cosmic-Time Generative Presentism (CTGP) holds that only the present exists, that physical reality is generated through successive stages, and that truths about the determinate past are grounded in primitive past-tensed properties of the present world. We develop a mathematical compatibility framework connecting these commitments to QRF covariance, stochastic quantum instruments, and relativistic measurement theory.
 
We first construct an explicit QRF-covariant, single-outcome quantum model using relational GRW-type events and a Lucretian truth profile. We establish a frame-independent determinacy criterion, characterize when different frame descriptions identify the same complete physical state and realized history, and prove covariance of the generative law and its history probabilities. We distinguish physical-state identity, historical identity, and observational indistinguishability, and show that the present quantum state need not determine the complete past. The construction demonstrates the joint mathematical consistency of these commitments without deriving their ontological interpretation or establishing physical adequacy.
 
We then formulate a compatibility program for relativistic quantum field theory, quantum clocks, and quantum gravity. For specified causally local instruments, we establish conditions for foliation-independent outcome histories and operational no-signaling. Free-field detector and Gaussian-collapse constructions identify both sufficient conditions for causal admissibility and obstructions to extending them to arbitrary local operations and to covariantly located collapses. We calculate the energy–momentum cost of Gaussian collapse and examine, without adopting it, a cosmological-time-guided candidate that defines normalized finite-volume dynamics but introduces preferred-frame structure.
 
To distinguish formal collapse prescriptions from physical implementations, we compare centre-time ordering, completion-time ordering, and a local field–pointer coupling. The pointer construction yields foliation-independent statistics, reproduces ordered-collapse predictions for causally orderable configurations with suitably prepared pointers, and defines a consistent law of outcome records even in spatially infinite universes. Interfering collapse regions, however, produce different joint outcome laws, showing that this local coupling does not in general reproduce a prescribed collapse ordering. Definite pointer readings require an explicit single-outcome postulate, and infinite-volume generation of the physical state remains open. We also give clock-neutral descriptions of late-record instruments for two ideal clocks and exhibit interference between alternative clock-dependent interaction orders.
 
These results establish a concrete mathematical compatibility framework, not a completed relativistic theory or an empirical confirmation of CTGP. They separate frame-dependent descriptions from the identity of realized events, identify which features of single-outcome dynamics survive changes of reference frame and foliation, and isolate the additional physical requirements for relativistic collapse, temporal quantum reference frames, and quantum gravity.
 
Within CTGP, σ is not a rendering engine that can independently pause or resume; it is a monotone parameter representing the universe's continuous generative ordering. A genuine halt of generation would not constitute an interval of paused physical time. A subsequent resumption would require an additional account of what makes that resumption possible. Thus, a literal stop-and-restart of reality's generation is not part of CTGP as currently formulated, whereas interruptions of particular physical processes do not necessarily interrupt the causal continuity of the universe itself.

In a block universe, if all existence could be mapped, then as you progress, everything the map refers to still exists, unchanged, as the content of the instant or state it was in when it was mapped. In CTGP, what you mapped, as you progress, existed when it was present and may still exist, changed; but the state you mapped no longer exists. What remains of that state are its consequences: present causal results, arrangements, and/or configurations, which may or may not still be recognizable or recoverable.

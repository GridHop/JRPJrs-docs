Deal's Worth — Part 8 of 9

"Its heart area has a barrier!" the armored warrior shouted. He pointed the axe toward the dragon's chest where a faint shimmer of transparent magical energy flickered whenever struck. "Aim for there!"

Jeff adjusted his approach, diving lower. He locked his double-jointed ring finger on his left hand into a hooked position. The tattoo on the back of his neck—the halo—began glowing, the intensity increasing with the amount of magic building up. Frost originated from his hands and spread rapidly across the creature's back where fire attacks had superheated the armor, causing it to glow red-orange. He controlled the frost's spread using his left thumb like a joystick, directing it to cover the heated areas precisely. The sudden temperature change created stress fractures with audible cracking sounds.

The dragon didn't flinch. It turned its head and looked at the frost crawling across its own back, quickly analyzing.

The armored warrior didn't waste the opportunity. He raised the axe overhead, spinning it in three complete rotations, then pointed it directly at the frosted areas. His lips moved rapidly, speaking particular words—his audible utterance. Lightning streaked from the black blades, targeting each frosted section. The combination of thermal stress and electrical assault shattered portions of the dragon's armor plating, leaving several open wounds that wept ichor.

Alicia sprinted to a new position, her breathing controlled despite the exertion. She found another crystal formation, this one tinted dark blue and purple, half again her height. Another gamble. She drew a large circle on it with her finger, placed both hands on it within the circle, and pressed hard on a facet angled toward the dragon's head. This time the gamble paid: from the crystal's far side, water erupted in a twisting stream, crossing the distance and breaking against the dragon's snout, sheeting across its eyes and nostrils in a clinging film. She shifted the angle of her pressure, walking the stream to follow its head as it moved.

Jeff flew in close, his sword drawn from its sheath at his side. The blade absorbed magic from him, becoming magical. He briefly unlocked his ring finger and re-locked his double-jointed index finger on his left hand. As he slashed with his blade, he exhaled sharply through his teeth—a quick "tssst" sound—and wind energy flowed through the metal, extending the cutting edge with compressed air. The enhanced strike bit deep into one of the open wounds on the dragon's flank.

The dragon roared—but it was not a cry. It was a weapon. The sound arrived as a physical wall, precisely aimed, battering Jeff end over end through the air. Beneath the roar, plate flaps along its spine were already standing up in a third configuration, and its exhale kept going, the roar sliding seamlessly into a shrieking pressure of compressed wind that Jeff barely rode out.

The creature planted all four legs wide, claws digging into stone, and began gathering power.

"Get clear! It's charging something big!" Alicia warned, abandoning the crystal as she prepared to move.

The armored warrior slammed his free fist against the axe's haft, then spread his arms wide while stomping his right foot. A barrier of crackling lightning poured out of the weapon and formed in front of him, a defensive lattice of electrical energy.

Deep in the dragon's throat, light was building, brighter with each passing second. The plates along its entire body lay flat and locked, streamlining it, bracing it.

"MOVE!" the armored warrior bellowed.

A massive beam of concentrated blue and cyan energy erupted from the creature's maw, directly in Jeff's flight path. The air itself seemed to tear apart from the sheer destructive power channeling through space.

Jeff's eyes widened. Too close. Moving too fast. No time to—

Lightning struck him.

The armored warrior, had noticed the danger Jeff was in, and had pointed the axe directly at Jeff, traced a quick zigzag pattern in the air with his other hand's index finger, and shouted a single sharp word. The bolt hit Jeff square in the chest, not to harm but to launch. The electrical force flung Jeff violently sideways, his body tumbling through the air away from the beam's path.

The dragon's beam scorched through the space where Jeff had been a fraction of a second before, the heat so intense that the air shimmered and distorted in its wake. The beam continued past, striking a sandstone formation in the distance and reducing it to molten slag.

Jeff crashed hard into the canyon wall, the impact knocking the wind from his lungs. He slid down several feet before catching himself with his wings, breathing hard, his chest aching from both the lightning strike and the collision.

The irises of his eyes shifted from glowing neon yellow to glowing orange.

"You... you could've warned me!" Jeff shouted at the armored warrior, anger rising in his voice.

"No time for warnings in battle!" the warrior called back, already moving to engage the dragon again. "You're alive. Focus!"

The dragon's attention fixed on Jeff, its massive head tilting with what might have been amusement. "Fragile creatures."

Pushing off from the wall, Jeff locked his double-jointed middle finger into position. Flames erupted from his hands, channeling through his sword's blade. He directed the fire's trajectory with his left thumb, sending a spiraling column of flame toward the dragon's face.

The dragon simply closed its eyes. The armored eyelids deflected the flames harmlessly.

"Fire took my eyes once. Several bodies ago," the dragon remarked, opening them again. "I corrected that."

Alicia reached another crystal formation, this one shot through with bright yellow. She used her finger to draw a circle on it, pressed both palms against it within the circle, fingers splayed, and pushed hard on a facet angled low, toward the dragon's rear left leg. A jagged arc of yellow electricity leapt from the crystal's far side, streaked across the canyon floor, and struck the limb—current coursing up through it, every muscle clenching at once. The crystal hummed under her hands as she held the pressure and the angle, feeding the arc. The dragon's leg buckled, its weight shifting.

The armored warrior saw the opening. He gripped the axe with both hands, held it horizontal at chest level, then thrust it forward while speaking three sharp syllables. A massive bolt of lightning tore from the black blades into the ground beneath the dragon's compromised leg. The earth exploded upward in a shower of rock and electrical discharge.

The dragon's leg gave way beneath it, and the creature adjusted, redistributing its weight across three limbs. Jeff flew in close again, his orange eyes blazing, slashing and stabbing with his sword, the fiery blade now cutting deep into an exposed wound. Ichor sprayed across his arms and chest.

The dragon's tail whipped around with devastating speed, the movement creating a sonic boom. Jeff barely managed to pull up, the tail passing inches beneath his feet. The wind from its passage spun him in a complete rotation before he regained control.

"Jeff! Its left wing joint!" Alicia shouted, pointing. "There's a gap in the armor!"

Jeff adjusted his flight path, but the dragon was faster than he anticipated. It did a massive swipe through the air, talons extended. Jeff twisted, his wings folding partially, but one talon caught his left wing's edge. Feathers scattered—some white, some now black.

Pain lanced through Jeff's wing. He dropped several feet before recovering, his flight now slightly uneven. His eyes flickered, the orange glow intensifying, and more black feathers began appearing among the white.

He locked his double-jointed thumb into a specific configuration while keeping his middle finger locked. The tattoo on his stomach—the yellow outlined smiley face with the red tear drop—began glowing. Dark energy and fire combined in his free hand, swirling together into a volatile sphere. With immense throwing force, he launched it directly at the dragon's wing joint.

The attack struck true, exploding against the gap in the armor. The joint gave with a wet crack, and the wing dropped, hanging at an awkward angle.

The dragon moved. Not toward Jeff, not toward the warrior—it turned and strode across the battlefield with sudden purpose, walking straight past two crystal formations without a glance, past a third, heading unerringly for a distant formation near the canyon wall—a towering mass twice the height of a man, pulsing with orange light.

The armored warrior went pale behind his visor. "It knows this valley!"

The dragon pressed its foreclaw against the orange formation and dragged one talon across the surface in a swift, practiced circle. It pressed a talon within the circle, angling with surgical precision. A massive beam of glowing orange energy erupted from the opposite side of the formation, sweeping across the battlefield in a horizontal arc.

Alicia dove behind a rock outcropping as the beam passed overhead, superheating the air. The armored warrior converted to his electrical form, dispersing into crackling energy that scattered in multiple directions before reforming in a safer location, the axe reforming in his grip with him.

Jeff flew upward hard, wings straining. The beam passed beneath him, close enough that he felt his skin burning from the radiant heat. Several more feathers—now a mix of white and black—singed and fell away.

His eyes shifted again. One iris glowed orange, the other glowed red.

The dragon turned its massive head toward Jeff. "Perhaps I should have devoured you first."

Jeff locked his double-jointed pinky and ring finger together in a complex configuration. Wind and darkness combined around him, the tattoo on the back of his neck—the halo—began glowing intensely, the ink blazing with light. His wind and darkness combination creating a vortex of cutting air and shadow energy. He directed it with both thumbs now, for more precise control.

The vortex slammed into the dragon's face, the wind blades cutting into its snout, the darkness corroding where it touched. The dragon's head snapped back from the force, and several scales cracked and fell away. Ichor ran freely down its jaw and it kept its eyes on Jeff.

Alicia saw her chance. She sprinted to a broad, red formation she hadn't yet tried, drew a large circle, pressed her hands against it within the circle, and pushed hard while dragging her left hand downward across its surface. The crystal answered with fire—not a beam, but a roaring, expanding cone of flame that howled out of the formation's far side like a furnace door thrown open. She angled it by adjusting her hands, walking the inferno across the dragon's damaged snout precisely where Jeff's attack had exposed flesh.

The flesh charred deep. The dragon's answering roar shook the canyon and crumbled smaller rock formations—but it was aimed, a concussive counterattack rolling over Alicia's cover, not a sound of suffering. Code doesn't suffer agony.

The armored warrior charged forward, the axe raised. He leaped high, spinning in midair while tracing a circle with the axe's blade. As he descended, he brought the weapon down in a powerful overhead strike enhanced with lightning. The black blades bit into the dragon's shoulder, electrical energy coursing through the wound.

The dragon thrashed—its body jerking where the current seized its muscles—and its tail swept in a wide arc, catching the armored warrior and sending him crashing into a canyon wall with enough force to crack the stone. He slumped to the ground, struggling to rise, the axe still locked in his fist.

"Two left," the dragon said.

Jeff's eyes changed again—both irises now glowing red, the pupils still black. More of his wing feathers had turned black, all of one wing's feathers now black.

"Alicia, get him to safety!" Jeff commanded, his voice tight with barely controlled rage.

Alicia ran toward the fallen warrior, but the dragon shifted its bulk, blocking her path.

The runes on Jeff's forearm spread further up his arm—new runes appearing that hadn't been there before, crawling across his skin like living script. He unlocked all his finger configurations and locked his double-jointed fingers in a different, more complex pattern that required both hands working in coordination.

Massive spikes of ice erupted from the ground beneath the dragon's feet, piercing up through the gaps in its armor. The creature's limbs locked and shuddered as the spikes ran them through—its body being physically pinned. Jeff wasn't finished. He adjusted his thumb positions while maintaining the finger locks, and lightning—not his normal magic, but something darker, tinged with shadow—struck down from above, hitting every ice spike simultaneously.

The corrupted electricity locked the dragon's muscles against the piercing cold. It broke free from several of the ice spikes anyway, tearing its own flesh to do it, ichor flowing freely from the wounds.

The dragon's eyes narrowed. Its plate flaps rose along its haunches in yet another arrangement, and it swung its tail toward a green-and-yellow tinted crystal formation nearby—another one it clearly knew. The tail's tip drew a swift circle against the crystal's surface, then pressed within it at an angle. From the formation's far side erupted a hissing torrent of green-yellow liquid, aimed not at Jeff but at Alicia.

Alicia threw herself to the side, rolling behind cover and it missed her by inches, stone dissolved into a steaming, collapsing ruin.

Jeff's eyes shifted again—the white parts of his eyes turned black, the irises remaining glowing red.

Jeff was no longer in control of his own body.

Jeff's body moved with inhuman precision and speed. He dove straight down toward the dragon, his wings—now completely black—folded tight against his back. At the last second, he spread his wings wide, creating a massive gust that kicked up sand and debris, temporarily blinding the dragon.

He landed on the dragon's back, between its shoulder blades. His sword plunged down into one of the open wounds, and he channeled everything through it—fire, ice, wind, lightning, and darkness all at once. The magical energies poured into the dragon's body, wreaking havoc on its internal organs.

The dragon bucked and twisted, trying to dislodge him.

Jeff's face was smiling.

He wrenched the sword sideways, expanding the wound. The dragon's movements grew more urgent—the fast, focused urgency of a mind watching its vessel fail and calculating what could still be done.

Alicia, seeing the dragon distracted, ran to the fallen armored warrior. She checked his breathing—still alive, but unconscious. She dragged him behind a larger rock formation, out of immediate danger.

Jeff pulled his sword free and drove it into a different wound, this one closer to the dragon's spine. He unlocked his finger configuration and formed a new one with just his right hand, his left maintaining his grip on the sword. The black runes on his forearm now covered his entire left arm.

A sphere of pure shadow formed in his right hand, dense enough that it seemed to pull light into itself. He pressed it against the wound where his sword pierced, and the darkness flowed into the dragon's body like poison.

The dragon's movements became more frantic—not desperate, but ruthless, spending its failing body like currency. It rolled onto its side, trying to crush Jeff against the ground.

Jeff's wings beat once, powerfully, launching him clear. He hovered in the air, his black-and-red eyes fixed on the creature. His sword was still embedded in the dragon's back.

"Fragile creature," Jeff's avatar said.

He locked all ten of his double-jointed fingers into a configuration so complex it looked painful. His tattoos that had glowed earlier now blazed with light—the halo on his neck, the "yellow outlined smiley face with the red tear drop" on his stomach.

The air around Jeff darkened, shadows gathering from every direction. Lightning crackled through the shadows, purple and black instead of blue and white. Ice crystals formed in the air, each one wreathed in dark fire. Wind howled, carrying all these elements together into a swirling maelstrom of destructive power.

The dragon dragged itself toward the nearest green crystal formation, one foreclaw stretching out, a talon already beginning the curve of a circle against its surface—

Jeff brought both hands down in a chopping motion.

The maelstrom descended.

Everything—shadow, lightning, ice, fire, and wind—slammed into the dragon simultaneously. The creature's remaining armor shattered like glass. Its scales peeled away. Its flesh burned and froze and electrified and cut all at once.

When the maelstrom dissipated, the dragon lay still, its body a ruined mess of torn flesh and broken plating, the talon resting an inch short of completing its circle.

Jeff hovered in the air, breathing heavily. His eyes remained black with red irises, his wings completely black. His tattoos that had been glowing still glowed, though the light was beginning to fade.

He descended slowly, landing on the ground near the dragon's head. He walked forward, his movements being controlled.

The dragon's eye opened slightly, its vast body barely clinging to life.

"You..."

Jeff raised his hand, fingers beginning to form another configuration.

Jeff's breathing slowed. The black in the whites of his eyes began to recede, color returning gradually. The red glow in his irises flickered.

Jeff's hand lowered.

Jeff's eyes returned fully to glowing orange, the black receding from the whites of his eyes. His wings began showing white feathers again among the black. Able to control his own body again, Jeff turned away from the dragon.

"Jeff!"

Alicia stepped out from behind the rock formation, leaving the unconscious warrior safely concealed.

Jeff's wings trembled. His eyes shifted from glowing orange to glowing neon yellow, then began to dim.

Jeff's legs buckled. Alicia rushed forward, catching him before he fell.

"I've got you," she said, supporting his weight. "I've got you."

Jeff's eyes completed their return to non-glowing dark brown. His wings—now half white feathers, half black feathers—drooped with exhaustion.

"The warrior..." Jeff managed to say.

"He's alive. Unconscious, but alive," Alicia assured him.

The armored warrior groaned from behind the rock formation. Alicia helped Jeff over to him, both of them exhausted...

Chapter 5: Corwin and Rook

The armored warrior's name, once Alicia pried it out of him between labored breaths, was Corwin. He said it like he expected them to recognize it. When they didn't, something in his shoulders sagged with the specific disappointment of a man who'd built an entire highlight reel in his head and just found out nobody was watching.

"You've never heard of me," he said. It wasn't a question. It was grief.

"We just watched you get backhanded into a canyon wall," Jeff said, still sitting because standing seemed like a personal attack on his body right now. "I feel like that's a pretty good introduction on its own."

"I softened it up first."

"Sure you did."

"I did! Look at it!" Corwin gestured weakly at the dragon's ruined bulk, forty feet of shattered plating cooling in the desert heat like a demolished cathedral. Ichor pooled black and slow beneath it, already crusting at the edges where the air met it. "You think it just decided to lose?"

"I think it looked at us and calculated that this was, statistically, its worst day," Alicia said, kneeling to check the burn along Corwin's ribs where his armor had buckled. "Which, respectfully, might not have anything to do with you."

Corwin opened his mouth to argue and then apparently remembered he had two broken ribs and closed it again.

Jeff wasn't listening anymore. He was looking at the dragon.

It hadn't gone still the way a killed thing goes still — the sudden absence, the weight dropping out of a body like someone unplugged it. This was slower. Its remaining eye, half-lidded, tracked nothing, but it hadn't glazed. Its chest, what was left of it, rose and fell in increments too small to be called breathing and too regular to be called anything else. Dying, if it was dying, was taking its sweet time about it, like a phone battery insisting it still had six percent left through pure spite.

"It talked like it's done this before," Jeff said.

"It walked straight to the right crystal every time like it had notes."

"It could be a wish," Corwin offered, wincing as he sat up. "Somebody wished a legendary creature into existence at some point. Won't die permanently any more than the rest of us do."

The dragon's eye rolled, slow as a tide, and fixed on Jeff.

"You," it said. The word came out wet, dragged over broken things in its throat. "I remember cursed possession like yours."

"I get that a lot," Jeff said.

Its eye slid shut, and this time it stayed shut, and the small increments of movement in its chest went from small to smaller to nothing, the way a held breath finally, quietly, gives up. It simply stopped, the way a machine stops when the last charge bleeds out of it, and the canyon held its silence like it was used to holding worse.

Something dropped out of that silence at an angle no hawk would have attempted and no sane creature would have survived, cutting across the sun close enough that Jeff saw its shadow before he saw it — a hard bank, a violent snap of wings that kicked up a fan of grit into his face, and then it was standing on the dragon's largest surviving horn like it had booked the spot in advance.

Bird wasn't quite the right word, though it was doing its best. Two feet of it, maybe a little more, feathers the restless black-teal of oil sitting on water, shifting color when it moved like it hadn't fully decided what color it wanted to commit to. Threaded through the black were narrow seams of gold, not painted on and not natural either — too clean, too deliberate, like filigree pressed into metal rather than grown. Its eyes were a pale storm-gray that didn't belong on anything with feathers, and they missed nothing, least of all the fact that everyone present was staring at it.

It looked down at the dragon's corpse with the specific unimpressed air of someone arriving after the meeting had already wrapped.

"You started without me," it said. The voice came out crisp and fully formed, every consonant landing exactly where it aimed to land — nothing like the wet drag the dragon had spent its last words on.

"You weren't invited to the part that mattered," Corwin said, grinning through what that grin clearly cost him. "You were invited to the flying-around part."

"I was extensively flying around. At altitude. Some of us appreciate a good canyon." It hopped once, resettled, and turned that gray gaze on Jeff and Alicia with open, unhurried assessment. "New faces. Wonderful. More witnesses."

"That's a wish-granter," Jeff said, in the tone of a man doing math he didn't like the answer to.

"That's your wish-granter," Alicia said to Corwin.

"Still on duty," the bird said, dipping its head in a bow that somehow communicated total formality and zero actual respect at the same time. "Two wishes down. One outstanding. Assuming he doesn't decide to sit on it, in which case I fly around some more and eventually go home."

"Genie," Jeff said, testing the word.
